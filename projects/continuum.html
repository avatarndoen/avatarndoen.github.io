Overview

After building the arm, I wanted to explore flexible manipulation, so I built an underactuated tendon-driven continuum robot. To make its motion easier to control, I designed the tendon routing so each actuator could bend its corresponding section while the others largely maintained their bending angles. I then used modeling and inverse kinematics to turn a desired configuration into actuator commands.

Mechanical Design

To make motion control easier, I routed the tendons so each actuator could bend its corresponding section while the other sections largely maintained their bending angles, producing motion similar to a parallel-linkage robotic arm. Alternating compliant joints, passive segments, and tendon attachments enable bending in two perpendicular planes, with a compliant gripper for manipulation.

Dynamic Modeling & Inverse Kinematics

I then needed to determine the tendon displacements required for a desired configuration. I developed a dynamic model to validate the mechanical design and used inverse kinematics to calculate actuator commands for open-loop control. I sent these model-based commands to the physical robot to demonstrate the tendon-routing behavior.

Trajectory Control & Motion Testing

With these commands, I tested predefined trajectories and needle pickup and insertion into a grape. The demonstrations use model-based open-loop commands to explore the mechanism’s motion and manipulation.

What Is Next?

Next, I want to use camera-based configuration estimation for closed-loop control, correcting differences between commanded and actual motion. Adaptive, obstacle-aware control is a further direction I want to explore. These are future extensions of the current open-loop demonstrations.
