Overview

For my undergraduate thesis at ITB’s Biomechanics Laboratory, I investigated how a compliant prosthetic wrist could be personalized to different body sizes while maintaining biomimetic motion and stiffness. Working independently with faculty guidance, I brought mechanical design, automated simulation, optimization, and prototype testing together to explore that question.

Mechanism Development

To study wrist motion and stiffness through the mechanism itself, I used compliant rolling-contact joints with asymmetric profiles. Stacked modules provide flexion–extension, while an orthogonal module adds radial–ulnar deviation. Changing these profiles let me explore how geometry affected movement in different directions.

Parametric Design

To personalize the wrist, I needed to vary its geometry consistently. I parameterized the overall size, rolling profiles, and flexure dimensions in a master sketch so I could generate and compare different designs automatically.

Automated CAD–CAE

Comparing many designs manually would have been impractical, so I connected automated CAD generation with finite-element analysis. I used geometry and face metadata to automate boundary conditions, contacts, meshing, solving, and extraction of motion, stiffness, and stress responses. This produced the simulation data I needed to compare designs and train surrogate models.

Experimental Setup

To check the optimized design’s motion, I built a printed prototype and a motor-driven tendon-pulling rig with inline force measurement. The measured range of motion differed from the design targets by less than 2% across the four tested directions.

Scalable Designs

I used optimization to generate wrist geometries for different body sizes while preserving their prescribed motion and stiffness targets. I then re-evaluated the optimized geometries using FEA to check the surrogate-based predictions.

Surrogate-Assisted Optimization

Searching for optimal geometries required more evaluations than I could practically simulate. To address this, I trained machine-learning surrogate models on the FEA results to predict how new designs would perform. I then combined these models with multi-objective optimization to approach the target motion and stiffness while meeting stress limits.
